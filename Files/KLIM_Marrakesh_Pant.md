## What it is

The Marrakesh Pant is KLIM's premium warm-weather riding trouser — a mesh-style pant made from 1000D Karbonite™ Micromesh Cordura® nylon with elastane woven in. Every single panel flows air, not just strategically placed mesh inserts. The result is arguably the most comfortable and breathable CE AA-rated motorcycle trouser available at any price.

Gen 2 (2023 onwards) was redesigned with a slimmer leg and more fitted cut than its predecessor — it now looks like a smart casual trouser off the bike as much as a riding pant on it. This is not a baggier off-road-style pant. It's slim, structured, and suited to road riding.

Despite feeling like you're wearing denim, it holds a CE AA rating under EN 17092-3. Motolegends note there are Gore-Tex trousers costing over £1,000 that don't rate this highly. Comfort and protection are not a trade-off here — you get both.

## Top selling points

1. **CE AA rated — without the waterproof membrane.** Most customers assume a mesh or air-flow pant trades down on protection. The Marrakesh is rated AA under EN 17092-3. Motolegends make the point clearly: there are Rukka and Klim Gore-Tex trousers costing over £1,000 that don't achieve this rating. That's a powerful line.

2. **Every panel flows air — not just mesh inserts.** Unlike typical "mesh" trousers with strategic vent panels, the Karbonite Micromesh Cordura is porous across the entire garment. Air flows at walking speeds. In warm weather, nothing on the road-riding market is more breathable at this price point.

3. **4-way stretch + D3O Ghost armour = wearing it all day.** The Ghost armour is the thinnest and most flexible protective insert D3O makes — combined with a fully stretch chassis, customers often describe this as the first motorcycle trouser that actually feels like wearing clothes. That comfort angle sells itself.

4. **Designed to be the jacket's perfect pair.** The Marrakesh jacket and pant share the same fabric DNA, aesthetic, and system philosophy. A customer buying the jacket should leave with this pant — they're better together, and the cross-sell conversation is natural.

5. **Three leg lengths.** Regular, Short, and Tall. This is often overlooked but matters enormously for taller or shorter riders who've always struggled with riding trousers pooling at the boot or not reaching the waist properly.

6. **Sized like jeans.** Waist measurement sizing (30 through 42 in Regular) removes the guesswork. Customers who know their jeans size know their Marrakesh size. Note: the waist cuts generous — recommend sizing down one.

7. **D3O Ghost armour is upgradeable.** For customers who want Level 2 protection, KLIM's Rogue™ armour slots straight in. Easy upsell opportunity, and useful for customers comparing the Marrakesh to higher-spec pants.

> ⚠️ **Staff notes — be upfront on these before the sale:**
>
> **1. No connecting zip.** The Marrakesh pant does not have a connecting zip to join with the jacket. Neither does the Marrakesh jacket by default. Motolegends can sew a zip into the jacket in-store; alternatively, a Halvarssons Waist Zip worn as a separate belt piece can connect the two halves. Don't let a customer discover this post-purchase.
>
> **2. Boot compatibility.** The leg opening will not go over full adventure boots with large plastic shin protectors. Short adventure boots (e.g. Forma ADV Tourer, Outlander GTX) are fine. Anything with a tall plastic shin guard — BMX-style ADV boots, Forma Terra Evo, Sidi Adventure 2 — will not fit inside the leg. This is the most common return trigger. Ask the customer what boots they're planning to wear.
>
> **3. Ghost armour can shift in loose-fit.** D3O IP Ghost is the thinnest armour KLIM uses. In a pant with some fit looseness, it can migrate under hard use. For customers planning any off-road or gravel riding, either recommend they upgrade to Rogue™ armour or suggest Endura Ghost knee pockets which clamp the pad more securely in place.
>
> **4. Not waterproof.** The DWR treatment handles light drizzle. In sustained rain, the customer needs a waterproof over-layer. The Scott Ergo Pro DP or similar is the standard recommendation. This is not a weakness — it's the design trade-off that lets the pant breathe so well.

## Common questions customers might ask + good answers

**"Is this just a mesh pant? Will it protect me?"**
It's rated CE AA under EN 17092-3 — the same standard that full-spec adventure trousers are rated to. Motolegends point out that some Gore-Tex trousers costing £1,000+ don't achieve that rating. The Ghost armour in knees and hips is Level 1; if they want Level 2 you can upgrade the inserts on the spot.

**"Will it keep me dry?"**
No — not in sustained rain. The DWR treatment sheds light drizzle but it's not waterproof. You'll need a separate waterproof oversuit or shell trousers over the top if rain is likely. For summer UK touring that's standard practice with this kind of pant.

**"Can I wear these with my [adventure boots]?"**
Depends on the boot. Short, low-profile adventure boots — yes. Full ADV boots with large plastic shin guards — no, the legs won't fit over them. Ask what boots they've got and check before purchase. This is the most common issue.

**"How do I size these?"**
Same as jeans — if you wear 34W jeans, go for size 34. The waist runs slightly generous, so if you're between sizes, go down. Three leg lengths available: Regular, Short, and Tall — measure inseam if unsure.

**"Do these zip to the jacket?"**
Not out of the box. The Marrakesh jacket doesn't have a connecting zip as standard either. We can sew a zip into the jacket, or you can use a Halvarssons Waist Zip as a connector piece.

**"What's the difference between this and the Carlsbad pant?"**
The Carlsbad is waterproof (Gore-Tex), warmer, and better for year-round use. The Marrakesh is not waterproof, flows much more air, and is purpose-built for warm weather. If they're riding primarily in summer or hot conditions, Marrakesh. If they want one pant that does all seasons, Carlsbad.
