# KLIM Induction Jacket

> **⚡ Don't confuse this with the Induction *Pro*.** The standard **Induction** (SKU 5060-003, **$449.99**) is a full-mesh summer jacket with **Level 1** D3O armour included. The **Induction Pro** (SKU 5056-xxx, $549.99) is a different, higher-spec jacket — Marrakesh-style stretch Cordura mixed with micro-mesh and **Level 2** D3O. Different price, different armour, different fit. See [Induction vs the family](#induction-vs-marrakesh-vs-induction-pro) below.

## What it is
The Induction is KLIM's dedicated hot-weather road mesh jacket — built to move as much air as possible while still passing full CE certification. The redesigned version is built around KLIM's Karbonite™ Mesh: an industrial-grade, high-strength open mesh that flows air through the whole chassis, with 500D Cordura® overlays on the high-wear areas and stretch panels for mobility. It comes fully armoured (Level 1 D3O all round, including a back pad) and is CE AA rated — putting it a clear step above the cheap "teabag" mesh jackets it superficially resembles. At $449.99 it's KLIM's most affordable way into a genuinely protective summer jacket.

## Who it's for
- Riders in hot climates who want **maximum airflow** and will still be covered by a real CE AA jacket
- Budget-conscious buyers who want KLIM protection at the lowest KLIM jacket price point
- Commuters and road riders who overheat in a laminated or stretch-Cordura jacket
- Anyone building a **layering system** — pair with the Zephyr Wind Shirt and Maverick Down to extend it into cooler weather
- **Not suited to:** cold or wet UK riding as a standalone jacket (it flows so much air you'll get cold fast — layer, or look at the Marrakesh); riders wanting the plushest feel or best layering stretch (the Marrakesh is more comfortable); serious off-road (that's the Baja S4); riders who want Level 2 armour as standard (that's the Induction Pro).

## Top selling points

1. **Most airflow in the KLIM road range, for the least money.** Open Karbonite™ mesh flows air through the entire jacket at any speed — the coolest-riding KLIM road jacket, and the cheapest KLIM jacket at $449.99.

2. **It's a real CE AA jacket, not a "teabag" mesh.** Cheap mesh jackets are flimsy and untested for abrasion. The Induction is certified CE AA to EN 17092-3 with 500D Cordura overlays — proper protection that just happens to breathe like a mesh jacket.

3. **Fully armoured out of the box — including a back pad.** Level 1 D3O at shoulders and elbows *plus* a D3O Viper Level 1 back pad included. Many rivals (and even the Marrakesh) don't include a back protector as standard.

4. **The gateway into KLIM.** Lowest price of entry to the brand's lifetime warranty, build quality and armour. A natural first KLIM jacket, and a natural companion to the Marrakesh for riders who want a dedicated hot-weather option.

5. **Layering-ready.** Sits at the top of KLIM's layering system — add the Zephyr Wind Shirt and a Maverick Down underneath to stretch its usable temperature range well down. Lead with this for UK buyers, because on its own it flows too much air once the sun drops.

## Common questions customers might ask + good answers

**"What's the difference between the Induction and the Induction Pro?"**
Both are hot-weather mesh jackets, both CE AA. The standard Induction ($449.99) has an open Karbonite mesh chassis with 500D Cordura overlays and comes with Level 1 D3O armour plus a back pad. The Pro ($549.99) mixes the Marrakesh's stretchy 1000D Cordura with micro-mesh, fits a bit closer, and comes with Level 2 armour. Pro = more protection and a more premium chassis; standard = more airflow-per-pound and a lower price.

**"How's it different from the Marrakesh?"**
The Marrakesh is stretch Cordura through every panel — more comfortable, best for layering, more understated looks — and it's not a mesh jacket. The Induction is actual open mesh, so it flows more air and costs a little less, and unlike the Marrakesh it includes a back pad. If comfort and one-jacket versatility matter most, Marrakesh. If cool airflow and price matter most, Induction.

**"Is it warm enough for UK riding?"**
On its own, only on genuinely warm days — it's built to flow air, so you'll get cold quickly when the temperature drops. The answer is layering: a Zephyr Wind Shirt under it, a Maverick Down below that for colder days, and a waterproof over the top when it rains. Layered up, it covers a wide range; as a standalone it's a summer jacket.

**"Is it waterproof?"**
No — it's a mesh jacket, the opposite of waterproof by design. That's the point: air flows straight through. If they need to stay dry, pair it with a waterproof over-layer, or look at the Carlsbad or Latitude (Gore-Tex laminated jackets).

**"What armour does it come with, and can I upgrade it?"**
It ships with D3O CE Level 1 at the shoulders and elbows and a D3O Viper Level 1 back pad — all included. The elbow armour is adjustable and everything is removable. You can upgrade to KLIM's Rogue™ armour in Level 1 or Level 2 if you want more protection.

**"Is the CE rating any good?"**
Yes — it's CE AA, certified to EN 17092-3. That's the same abrasion-resistance rating as the Marrakesh and most of the KLIM road range, and it's high for a jacket that flows this much air. The armour is graded separately: Level 1 as supplied, upgradeable to Level 2.

**"Will it fit me?"**
KLIM's road jackets run to a relatively narrow set of sizes, so fit is the one thing to check in person rather than assume. Try it on with any layers you'd realistically wear underneath.
