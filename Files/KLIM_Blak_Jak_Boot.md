## What it is
The Blak Jak is KLIM's street and casual motorcycle boot — a CE-certified classic leather work boot with integrated motorcycle-specific features kept deliberately subtle. No aggressive styling, no obvious buckles or plastic panels. It looks like a quality leather work boot you'd wear all day; the CE certification, asymmetrical shifter pad, and XRD ankle pads are integrated without visual interruption.

The Gen 2 (3990-001) is a redesigned second generation with perforated leather for breathability, a welted rubber outsole, and a CE 1221 rating — meaning it scores Level 2 on abrasion resistance and Level 2 on transverse rigidity, the highest levels for those measures. The GTX variant (3490-000) adds Gore-Tex waterproofing and uses full-grain leather with a bonded rubber outsole construction, at $259.99.

Neither has a BOA system — both use traditional metal lace eyelets. This gives a more classic boot aesthetic at the cost of BOA's precision and speed.

KLIM position the Blak Jak as "the boot you'll wear all day, every day" — it's designed to live equally in the pub, the office, and on the bike.

**Shared (both variants):**

- **Sole board:** Midweight lasting board — provides riding and walking comfort; not as stiff as the Outlander's polycarbonate board, appropriate for a street boot
- **Ankle protection:** 3mm XRD® Impact Protection perforated foam pads — at the tongue and ankle; XRD absorbs impact energy, perforated for breathability
- **Gearchange pad:** Asymmetrical — left boot only; protects the leather from repeated shifter contact; low-profile so it's not visible unless looking for it
- **Eyelets:** Metal lace eyelets — traditional construction; lace-up closure, not BOA

- **Logos:** Low profile embossed — intentionally subtle; this is not a boot that announces itself

## Top selling points

1. **CE-certified motorcycle boot that looks like normal footwear.** The Blak Jak's central value proposition is invisibility. Most customers in a pub or coffee shop won't identify it as a motorcycle boot. The shifter pad, XRD ankle pads, and reinforced toe/heel are all present — all integrated without advertising themselves. For street riders who hate looking like they've just stepped off an enduro course, this matters.

2. **CE 1221 on the non-GTX — better abrasion and rigidity rating than many motorcycle-specific boots.** The 1221 rating means Level 2 abrasion resistance and Level 2 transverse rigidity. The Outlander GTX, for comparison, rates 1112 — Level 1 in both those areas. The Blak Jak non-GTX is a stiffer, more abrasion-resistant boot at those test points, even without waterproofing. For a street rider who cares about slide protection rather than off-road capability, the Blak Jak's CE numbers are more relevant.

3. **XRD ankle and tongue pads — not just stiffeners.** The 3mm XRD foam at the ankle and tongue does impact energy absorption, not just padding for comfort. Both variants share this. It's a meaningful spec detail that distinguishes this from work boots with no impact protection at the malleolus.

4. **Full-grain leather on the GTX.** The GTX uses full-grain leather — the highest quality grade, where the outer surface of the hide is retained. Most affordable leather goods use split or corrected grain. Full-grain leather wears better, develops patina, and has better natural abrasion resistance. Combined with Gore-Tex and CE certification, the GTX is a genuinely premium everyday riding boot.

5. **The non-GTX perforated leather is not a compromise — it's the design.** A customer comparing the non-GTX to the GTX might assume "no waterproofing = cheaper/worse." The non-GTX is specifically designed without waterproofing to allow breathability through the perforations. For summer commuters or riders in dry climates, a waterproof membrane traps heat. The non-GTX is the right product for the right conditions.

## Common questions customers might ask + good answers

**"Is this the same as a Chelsea boot or work boot — does it have any actual protection?"**
It has CE motorcycle certification. Reinforced toe and heel, 3mm XRD foam at both ankles and tongue, and an asymmetrical gearchange pad are all integrated. The CE 1221 rating on the non-GTX means Level 2 abrasion resistance (the highest level) and Level 2 transverse rigidity — stiffer and more abrasion-resistant at those tested points than many adventure boots. It doesn't look like a motorcycle boot, but it is one.

**"Why is the non-GTX cheaper — is it a worse boot?"**
No — different design for different conditions. The non-GTX is specifically designed to be breathable. The perforated leather allows airflow that a waterproof-membrane boot cannot provide. In summer or dry conditions, the non-GTX is the better choice. The GTX adds Gore-Tex waterproofing and full-grain leather at $40 more, which is relevant for wet weather. Neither is inferior — they're designed for different use cases.

**"What's the welted outsole on the non-GTX?"**
Welted construction means the outsole is stitched to a welt (a thin strip of leather or rubber around the perimeter) rather than simply glued. It's a traditional quality construction method — the outsole is more securely attached, and in premium boots it can be resoled. The GTX uses bonded (glued) construction instead — common in waterproof boots where stitching would create holes that compromise the membrane.

**"Why no BOA dial — the Outlander has one?"**
The Outlander is an adventure boot designed for speed and off-road use; BOA fits that context. The Blak Jak is a work/street boot — traditional metal eyelets match the aesthetic and context. A BOA dial on what's supposed to look like a classic leather work boot would undermine the design intent. The trade-off is that entry and lacing are slower than BOA.
