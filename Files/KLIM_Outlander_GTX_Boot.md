## What it is
The Outlander GTX is KLIM's short adventure motorcycle boot — designed to a very specific brief: motorcycle protection at ankle height, full waterproofing, and walking comfort good enough to hike in after you've ridden there. At $299.99 (SKU 3926-001), it uses leather (a Gen 2 upgrade over the original synthetic construction), Gore-Tex waterproofing, a BOA Fit System with sequential lacing, 5mm XRD ankle pucks, a polycarbonate lasting board for sole stiffness, and an aggressive Hypergrip-style sole with cutouts for the footpegs.

Motolegends describe it as unlike any other short adventure boot on the market — because it was designed from a blank sheet, not cut down from a tall boot. They stock all four colourways, have a written review, and a dedicated YouTube video — one of the strongest editorial endorsements in KLIM's footwear range.

## Top selling points

1. **It actually walks.** Most short motorcycle boots are compromises — adequate on the bike, miserable in a mountain village. The Outlander was designed with a specific brief to be genuinely walkable. The Hypergrip sole provides real off-road grip. The leather shell and XRD padding move with the foot. Motolegends describe it as "part-walking boot and part-motorcycle boot" — and mean it as a compliment, not a criticism of either capability.

2. **BOA lacing on a motorcycle boot.** Traditional buckles and laces on adventure boots are a frustration. The BOA dial system tightens the boot evenly, precisely, and quickly — and opens the shaft far wider than traditional laces do. Getting in and out is dramatically faster and easier, which matters for real adventure riding: setting up camp, walking terrain, changing back and forth. The BOA Lifetime Guarantee means the dials and lace are covered for the life of the boot.

3. **Gen 2 leather vs. Gen 1 synthetic.** The original Outlander was protective but stiff, required break-in time, and wasn't always comfortable. The leather Gen 2 is pliable from the first wear. Motolegends note it's "more pliable, softer to the touch and more comfortable" while being equally protective. Customers who tried the first generation and didn't get on with it should try Gen 2 before writing the model off.

4. **XRD replacing D3O at the ankles — a meaningful comfort improvement.** The Gen 1 Outlander used D3O ankle cups, which can be uncomfortably stiff in cold weather before they absorb foot heat. XRD ankle pucks are softer in normal use — no cold-morning rigidity — and provide equivalent or better impact protection. The 3mm XRD tongue also improves on D3O in the same way.

5. **CE certified to EN 13634:2017 — a proper motorcycle boot standard.** The Outlander is certified to Level 1112: Height 1, Abrasion 1, Rigidity 1, and Level 2 ankle/malleolus protection. The Level 2 ankle rating is the highest available for that measurement — the boot takes ankle protection seriously. WR (water resistant) and WAD (wide access design via BOA) certifications are also included.

6. **Four colourways on Motolegends.** All four KLIM colourways — Stealth Black, Chocolate Brown, Castlerock–Petrol, Asphalt–High Risk Red — are stocked by Motolegends. Customers who want a matching adventure system have real options.

> ⚠️ **CE boot standard explained for staff:** The EN 13634:2017 standard for motorcycle boots uses four levels (e.g., 1112). Position 1 = shaft height (1 = lower, 2 = taller). Position 2 = abrasion resistance. Position 3 = transverse rigidity. Position 4 = ankle/malleolus protection. The Outlander is 1112: height 1 (short cuff), abrasion 1, rigidity 1, ankle 2 (highest level). The "WR" confirms water resistance is tested; the "WAD" confirms the wide access design (BOA) meets the standard.

## Common questions customers might ask + good answers

**"How does this compare to a proper off-road boot like the Adventure GTX?"**
Different tools for different use cases. The Adventure GTX ($499.99) is taller, more rigid, higher in the range for technical off-road — proper dirt track and enduro use. The Outlander is a short adventure boot optimised for mixed use: road riding, some light off-road, and walking around. If a customer spends 80% of their time on roads and gravel with occasional off-road, the Outlander is the better choice. If they're doing technical trail riding, they need the Adventure GTX or a dedicated enduro boot.

**"Is it actually waterproof in heavy rain?"**
Yes — Gore-Tex construction, same standard as their touring gloves. No short boot is as watertight as a tall boot because water can enter over the top of the cuff in very heavy rain or deep puddles. But for rain riding, it will keep feet dry. If a customer is going to be riding through rivers or in very deep standing water, a tall boot is the answer.

**"Why does it have a BOA dial instead of laces or buckles?"**
BOA is faster, more precise, and easier than both. You can tighten and loosen in seconds, in gloves, without stopping. The sequential lacing (multiple eyelets) distributes pressure more evenly than a single lace-pull. The shaft also opens much wider with BOA than with buckles, making the boot dramatically easier to get into — especially important on a long day in the saddle when feet swell. The BOA dials and lace are covered by a lifetime guarantee.

**"What's the difference between Gen 1 and Gen 2?"**
Gen 1 used synthetic materials — functional but stiff, required break-in, heavier feel. Gen 2 uses leather — immediately more comfortable, more pliable, same level of protection. Gen 1 had D3O in the ankles and tongue — which can be hard and stiff in cold weather until it warms up. Gen 2 has XRD foam — softer in normal use, equally protective on impact. Gen 2 is a meaningfully better boot, not just a cosmetic update.
