## What it is
The Adventure GTX Short Glove is KLIM's short-cuff waterproof glove for adventure and all-season riders who want a compact, under-jacket-cuff fit without sacrificing leather construction or CE certification. It uses goat leather with stretch woven panels, a laminated Gore-Tex + Gore Grip membrane, a carbon fibre knuckle protector over PORON® XRD® foam, and CE Level 1 KP certification to EN 13594:2015.

KLIM calls it "the ultimate world-traveling, all-terrain waterproof leather glove." Motolegends stock it in Black, Asphalt Grey, Monument Grey, and Tan, and describe it as "more than the sum of its parts... it just kind of works."

The product niche it occupies is genuinely rare. Most waterproof gloves at this price are either fabric-shell or long-cuff gauntlet designs. A short-cuff, leather, waterproof, CE-certified glove is a small category — and the Adventure GTX Short Glove is the leading option in it.

## Who it's for
- Adventure and touring riders who want a leather waterproof glove that tucks under the jacket cuff
- All-weather or year-round commuters who want the feel of leather with confirmed waterproofing

## Top selling points

1. **The rare combination: leather + short-cuff + waterproof + CE certified.** Motolegends put it simply: *"The Adventure GTX is a rare commodity. It's a waterproof, shortie, leather, summer glove. That's not what everybody wants, but if this what you're after, then you won't find anything better."* The specific customer who needs a waterproof glove that tucks under the jacket cuff, in leather, with CE certification, has almost nowhere else to go.

2. **Laminated Gore-Tex — better feel than a loose liner.** The membrane is bonded directly to the leather outer. In a standard 2-layer waterproof glove, the Gore-Tex sits as a separate inner sock and moves against the leather — reducing feel and feedback. In the Adventure GTX Short, the lamination means the leather and membrane move as one piece. Riders notice the difference in dexterity and tactile feedback from the bars.

3. **Two-layer knuckle protection in a compact package.** Carbon fibre hard shell on the outside, PORON® XRD® foam on the inside — the same dual-layer approach used in the Induction Glove's knuckle. Hard shell spreads and redirects impact, XRD foam absorbs the residual energy. Most waterproof gloves at this price rely on softer armour inserts only.

4. **Works in genuine bad weather.** Customer reviews on Motolegends specifically call out multi-day touring in sustained heavy rain — "apocalyptic rain in Ireland" is one reviewer's phrase. These are not fair-weather waterproof gloves. The laminated construction means no wet-out through the membrane under extended soaking, and the Gore Grip prevents slippery grip in the wet.

5. **Leather that breaks in.** The initial snugness is the leather doing what leather does — it will conform to the rider's hand over 4–6 weeks of regular riding. Customers who return after break-in routinely say they've become their go-to glove. Setting this expectation at point of sale is the key staff job here.

## Common questions customers might ask + good answers

**"How is this different from the Induction Glove?"**
The Adventure GTX Short adds Gore-Tex waterproofing and a laminated membrane. The Induction has no waterproofing but is heavily perforated and significantly cooler in hot weather. The knuckle protection is similar (hard outer shell + XRD foam on both). At $119.99 vs $149.99, the Adventure GTX Short is actually cheaper — the difference is weather capability vs ventilation. If the customer rides in all conditions, the Adventure GTX Short. If they ride purely in summer and keep a waterproof glove for rain, the Induction + a separate waterproof option.

**"Is the waterproofing reliable?"**
Yes — Gore-Tex is laminated (bonded) to the leather outer, not a separate inner liner. Customer reviews from Motolegends specifically describe multi-day touring in sustained heavy Irish rain with completely dry hands. This is proper waterproofing, not just DWR coating.

**"Why does it feel so tight in the store?"**
Leather. It's normal — the gloves are cut snug because they'll expand and conform over 4–6 weeks of regular wear. Ask the customer to get their fingers fully seated in the finger tubes. If there's any room at all in the fingers, the fit is probably right. Feeling tight over the knuckles out of the box is expected.

**"What's the cuff design — will it work with my jacket?"**
It's a short glove designed to sit under the jacket cuff — the jacket sleeve goes over the top. It's not a gauntlet or long-cuff design. For riders who prefer the jacket sleeve to go inside the glove cuff (over-the-cuff style), the Vanguard GTX Long is the KLIM waterproof glove to look at instead.

**"Is there a women's version?"**
Yes — the Women's Adventure GTX Short Glove (SKU 5027-001) is built specifically for women's hands, not just a resized men's glove. It uses the same core construction. Check current stock and sizing in-store.

**"What's the CE rating?"**
CE Level 1 KP, certified to EN 13594:2015. KP stands for knuckle protection — this specifically certifies the carbon fibre knuckle protector meets the Level 1 standard. Same CE rating as the Induction and Dakar Pro gloves.
