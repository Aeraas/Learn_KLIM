## What it is
The Dakar Glove is KLIM's off-road and dual-sport entry glove — a redesigned MX-style glove built for riders who prioritise mobility, grip, and ventilation over leather construction or CE certification. It uses AX™ Suede on the palm and fingers, a stretch corded nylon backhand, and TPR (thermoplastic rubber) knuckle protection. At $44.99 it's the most accessible KLIM glove in the range, and a natural match for customers buying the Dakar Jacket and Pant who want a budget-appropriate full Dakar Series kit.

Motolegends do not stock the Dakar Glove. No Motolegends editorial content exists for this product.

## Who it's for
- Off-road and trail riders who need a light, mobile glove and don't require CE certification
- Customers completing the Dakar Series look (jacket + pant + glove) at an accessible price
- Riders who find leather gloves too stiff for off-road grip and control work
- Dual-sport riders doing primarily off-road sections who want maximum feel from the bars
- **Not suited to** road-only riders who need CE certification (direct to Dakar Pro or Induction), cold or wet conditions, or customers who want leather construction at this price point

## Top selling points

1. **$44.99 entry point into the KLIM glove range.** For a customer buying a Dakar Jacket ($179.99) who also needs a glove, the Dakar Glove completes the Dakar Series kit without doubling the budget. The price positions it as an honest no-frills choice rather than a compromise.

2. **Redesigned for more coverage and comfort.** KLIM specifically note "improved" across four areas in the current version: TPR knuckle coverage, neoprene cuff, Velcro closure, and pull tab entry. Customers who found the previous Dakar Glove stiff at the cuff or sharp-edged at the wrist strap should be told these have been addressed.

3. **Hourglass finger construction + silicone grip.** The hourglass pattern prevents finger fabric bunching during clutch and brake operation — a common problem in cheaper gloves. Combined with the silicone grip print on the palm and two fingers, the Dakar Glove offers positive bar feel without the weight or stiffness of a heavier glove.

4. **Seamless wrapped fingertips.** The fingertip seam is one of the most common comfort complaints in off-road gloves — it creates a pressure point against the bar or controls. Wrapping the fingertips eliminates this.

5. **Wide colour range including Dakar Series colour matching.** The Dakar Glove is available in colours that match the Dakar Jacket and Pant (e.g. Striking Petrol, although this is now closeout). For customers building a coordinated look, checking current in-stock colours against available jacket/pant options is a useful conversation.

## Common questions customers might ask + good answers

**"What's the difference between the Dakar Glove and the Dakar Pro?"**
Different categories entirely. The Dakar Glove is an off-road/MX glove with synthetic suede, TPR knuckle protection, and no CE certification — $44.99. The Dakar Pro is a dual-sport glove with goat leather, XRD® foam protection across knuckle, palm, fingers, and thumb, and CE Level 1 KP certification — $84.99. The Dakar Glove prioritises lightness and feel for off-road riding; the Dakar Pro adds leather abrasion resistance and certified protection for riders who also spend time on the road.

**"Is this CE certified?"**
No — the Dakar Glove does not carry a CE certification to EN 13594. It has TPR knuckle protection but this has not been tested to the KP standard. If CE certification is important to the customer, the Dakar Pro or Induction Glove are the right choices.

**"What is AX™ Suede?"**
It's a high-abrasion synthetic suede used widely in off-road and MX gloves. It's not leather, but it's durable and provides good feel on the controls. At this price point, synthetic construction is the norm — leather construction starts in the Dakar Pro range.

**"What's the TPR knuckle guard?"**
TPR is thermoplastic rubber — a soft, flexible impact material. It provides some protection from knocks and impacts at low speed, and covers a wider area of the knuckle than the previous version. It's not a hard shell (like the carbon fibre on the Induction), so it flexes with the hand. For off-road use where hand flexibility matters more than hard crash protection, this is appropriate. For road riding, the XRD foam and hard shell on the Induction is a stronger choice.

**"What colours match the Dakar Jacket?"**
The Dakar Jacket comes in Striking Petrol, Potter's Clay/Monument, and Monument Grey. Striking Petrol was available on the Dakar Glove but is now a closeout colour — check in-store stock. The safest current match is Black or Asphalt/Slate Gray. Golden Brown/Petrol could coordinate with a Potter's Clay jacket colourway.
